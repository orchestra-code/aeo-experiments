The manipulation worked precisely where it was applied and bought nothing elsewhere. One flag dominates the unstratified column: mat emits comfort language in 45% of prompts vs the human panel's 12%. Excluding it, the two panels are effectively tied on the unstratified flags — the honest statement is that constraining six dimensions did not improve the other nine, not that it degraded them.

### Is the Apple gap a brand-naming artifact? No.

Prompts naming the Apple ecosystem, by arm: {'hum': 7, 'mat': 0, 'neu2': 0}
hum apple share | naming prompts: 0.686 (n=7 prompts); | other prompts: 0.451 (n=136)

Dropping every brand-naming human prompt moves mat's H2 MAD 0.0893 -> 0.0869. The residual is a property of what human phrasings ASK FOR, not of what they name.


## B. Does conditioning on sub-intent close the gap?

H3' answers this in Jaccard; this restates it in share units. If a
client names its sub-intents and we generate prompts for those, the
relevant comparison is human vs synthetic prompts carrying the SAME
flag — the panel mix is then given, not estimated.

                  n_hum  n_mat  basket    mad     lo     hi  rank_tau
flag                                                                 
f_comfort            17     25       8  0.110  0.080  0.181     0.929
f_usage_music       102     41       6  0.110  0.076  0.156     0.733
f_travel_context    116     46       6  0.085  0.065  0.128     0.867

Conditioning does NOT shrink the gap: the within-sub-intent MADs sit
at or above the unconditional 0.089. The H2 failure is therefore not
primarily a mix artifact — something about synthetic phrasing shifts
the proportions in a consistent direction within every stratum.


## C. Pool membership or consistency?

Panel share pools a binary per response, so a gap can mean either
'fewer prompts surface the brand at all' (pool) or 'they surface it
less consistently across the 5 runs' (consistency). These are very
different claims commercially.

No tail brand is returned by any meaningful share of human prompts in all five runs. Tail instability is a property of the medium, not of synthetic panels — and mid-frequency head brands are barely more stable: a share of 0.45 that flips run-to-run within most prompts is not a stable brand fact being missed, it is a coin flip being averaged.


## What this layer supports

A sub-intent-matched synthetic panel is a defensible instrument for
**which brands belong to a use case, and roughly in what order**. It
is not one for **what percent of the time** — and part of that metric
is noise the human panel does not clear against itself. The tail is
unstable for every panel, human included.
